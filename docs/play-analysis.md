# Visual play timing analysis

Run metadata analysis once, then opt into native-frame play analysis:

```sh
just auto VIDEO
just play VIDEO --segment 1
just play VIDEO --all
```

`--segment` selects a 1-based entry in the saved `analysis.json` segment list, and defaults to 1. This does not download media or rerun metadata recognition. A matching play result is reused without frame decoding or OCR; `--force` rebuilds it. Completed output is preserved if a new build fails. `--all` analyzes every saved metadata segment and writes a linked `play/index.html` overview, continuing past individual failures and reporting them at the end. It cannot be combined with `--start` or `--duration`.

Open `.local/data/SOURCE_ID/play/segment-001/report.html` locally. The report embeds its data and uses no external scripts, fonts or services. It includes:

- the original recording, with seek links from the charts and event table;
- a vertical lane chart with earlier notes above later notes, showing target and input ranges;
- a note-by-note comparison table, including unmatched targets, and two aligned range bars for the selected note;
- combo and judgment-word refresh brackets, their evidence source and timing offsets;
- coverage, unreadable digits, excluded trajectories and ambiguous events;
- a receptor/combo overlay and an editable, downloadable layout;
- display-latency correction and relative median centering.

If the browser cannot read the relative media path, select the original file in the page. Playback depends on browser codec/container support. The tool never transcodes, trims, retimes or overwrites that recording. The page does not upload selected files.

## Meaning of the measurements

This reconstructs visible supported-color note heads from the recording. It does not load an authoritative chart or recover unseen notes or beat positions. Visible long-note bodies can supply start/end ranges; obscured or unreliable endpoints remain unresolved.

Interrupted same-lane head fragments can be joined when their original observations follow one linear path across at most 350 ms, the predecessor and successor are unique, and the combined track passes the normal motion and receptor-proximity checks. Competing continuations, other lanes and inconsistent positions stay separate. Joined notes retain their source fragment indices and all original time/position observations.

For each usable note track, a local linear fit predicts when its center crosses the observed receptor row. A confirmed combo change is bracketed between the last frame with the old counter and the first frame with the new counter. Two observations must confirm each counter value. Judgment words are observed independently of the counter, including before combo 4 and after a combo break. Matching uses those time intervals, never fabricated subframe input timestamps.

The default association tolerance is 45 ms around the observed update interval. A monotone sequence alignment compares rows and counter updates in time order, so close successive notes do not all get discarded merely because their search windows overlap. Near-equal competing alignments remain excluded. The counter advances per judged row, not per head: a multi-head chord shares one increment and one observed bracket. If a counter jump spans multiple consecutive rows, every associated row retains the original aggregate bracket; individual timestamps are never invented. The row span must fit inside the observed bracket plus 20 ms of fitting tolerance. Unassigned updates remain visible in a separate evidence table. Rolls, resets and observation gaps remain excluded from discrete input matching. Long notes are modeled separately as sustained states. Implausible large jumps and high-valued backwards digit reads do not replace the stable counter baseline. A long colored component reaching the receptor region blocks timing matches in its interval (with a 100 ms guard), because hold ticks and dense overlapping stacks cannot be attributed safely. A reset is not proof of a missed note. Sparse results indicate limited visual evidence, not a lack of player inputs.

The report separates reconstructed note heads, heads with timing evidence, and matched counter refreshes. It also reuses confirmed result-panel judgments as a whole-song reference when available, without extra OCR. Judgments include hold ticks, so their total is not a note-head count or a coverage percentage. A short analysis window still shows the whole-song reference. Missing matches are analysis gaps, not evidence of absent inputs.

## Long notes and sustained recognition

Dim, brief receptor pulses can support an already reconstructed hold when all three observations agree: a rise of at least 0.16 above the lane's recent baseline, light above the other lanes, and a connected body at the receptor. A positive combo or PERFECT/GREAT/GOOD/BAD update must occur within 40 ms. Such context can retain a single native frame; shared flashes, static light, MISS and missing bodies cannot. Each recovered span records the corroborating update indices/ranges and original source PTS. This hold-only evidence does not relax tap onset detection or invent a press/release time.

A long note is one body from its head to its tail. Holding the panel is sufficient during that body; repeated combo or judgment ticks are not separate presses. The analyzer acquires small native-frame color/body and receptor features once, without OCR, preserving every saved source PTS. Narrow colored vertical stripes distinguish bodies from separated tap heads. Body approaches and tails are fitted locally; a saved tail-shaped arrow track can corroborate the body's lower edge. Repeated fragments sharing a head or tail are deduplicated. Unsupported motion or clipped endpoints remain explicitly censored.

The chart shows a long body in its lane color with a square start note and end cap. A question mark identifies an unresolved endpoint, rather than an invented target or release time. Magenta spans beside it show a filled receptor within the reconstructed hold interval. Receptor observations come from every native frame, including neighboring frames where a flash or overlay hides the body's color; a body box is not required in the same frame as recognition. Only directly supported recognition spans are drawn. The overall recognition envelope is retained in JSON for audit and is not rendered as a dashed range; gaps are not measured switch transitions. This is visual evidence of hold recognition, not a physical pedal sensor. A player may already be holding before the head, and may keep holding after the tail. Physical press and release times therefore stay `null`.

Hold heads and tail fragments are removed from the discrete-press view. Counter or word updates during an ongoing hold cannot establish a new tap merely because their times are close: another lane needs its own receptor onset to support a separate tap candidate. Existing native detections and tap associations remain preserved in analysis for audit, with a separate `hold_analysis` reconstruction. The HTML projects them into a hold-aware chart and does not count hold ticks as independent presses.

`hold-frames.jsonl` preserves per-frame body measurements, source PTS, per-lane receptor states and native white-area fractions. Fractions allow receptor interpretation to change without reacquiring pixels. Legacy boolean-only traces receive a single native receptor-strip top-up in `receptor-features.npz`, independently keyed by source, bounds, geometry, measurement encoding and original timestamp digest; this pass performs no OCR. Shared full-grid whitening and its decay are unknown input evidence, rather than simultaneous presses on empty lanes. Strong light above the shared baseline can remain lane-local evidence. An unknown interval cannot establish a fresh independent input onset. `hold-features.json` identifies the source, bounds, ROI, geometry and detector format. `holds.json` records reconstructed long notes, covered fragments, endpoint ranges and sustained recognition spans. Changing geometry/source/detector encoding reacquires these features; changing hold reconstruction reuses them. Metadata OCR, counter templates and word templates are reused. A first upgrade requires a separate small-frame decode pass; matching subsequent runs do not decode again.

## Judgment words without a visible counter

PERFECT, GREAT, GOOD, BAD and MISS use a separate native-frame region. An optional `judgment_roi` layout field overrides the automatically derived region; the default includes the expanded initial word animation. At most 24 representative word crops receive English OCR, balanced across observed text colors. Exact vocabulary repeated across distinct source timestamps can corroborate stylized low-confidence text. Learned word shapes and colors are then matched by normalized contours, without per-frame OCR. When a touching note corrupts the word component, a bounded search compares the word itself at several animation scales. At least three of four horizontal word regions must retain their letter shapes; a small fragment or solid matching color cannot establish a label. Font templates are reusable across unchanged source media. Unknown words remain unknown, and the report lists unlearned labels.

A new label, reappearance after a confirmed blank, or a sudden proportional scale restart can establish a refresh. Two source frames confirm it. A persistent PERFECT is not counted repeatedly; unreadable/occluded frames are not assumed blank. Scale restarts require adjacent visible observations, consistent geometry and confirmation. Onsets with more than 100 ms of observation uncertainty remain unscored.

Mutually unique overlapping counter/word refresh brackets are paired first. Confirmed pairs anchor the remaining monotone alignment, preventing neighboring judgments from shifting onto an already assigned row. Counter and word order must agree. Remaining judgment rows are aligned between these anchors and merged with counter evidence. One note is counted once, with `combo`, `combo+judgment` or `judgment` provenance. Competing counter evidence and hold/dense-stack intervals remain excluded. MISS is observed failure evidence, never a press timestamp or timing-offset sample. A unique unmatched visible row in a bounded preceding window may be listed as a possible miss; dense ambiguous cases retain only the observed MISS event.

An already matched single-row counter update can also receive a label from at least two consistent, high-confidence word observations within 50 ms after its first new frame. Two agreeing reads must be no more than 35 ms apart. Unreadable later frames do not invalidate already confirmed letters or vote against a label. The window stops before the next observed counter transition; conflicting words, MISS, sparse observations and aggregate increments remain unlabeled. This supplies a label only: it does not create a word refresh or narrow the original timing interval. These matches retain the contributing source PTS in `judgment_observations`, use `judgment_label_source: post_combo_frames`, and have no `judgment_event`. The HTML marks them as `word frames` and explicitly shows `Judgment unknown` for unresolved labels. Compatible saved word observations are reused without decoding or OCR.

`judgments.jsonl` retains word observations, confidence, bounding boxes and original source PTS. `evidence/judgments/` contains sampled crops, OCR provenance, font templates and compressed native color features. Word detection/refresh changes can reuse those features and existing fonts without decoding or OCR; cached word classifications are invalidated when the reader changes. This path analyzes the current recording without creating or requiring a persistent chart catalog. The HTML shows the judgment and evidence source for matched heads, plus all observed word refreshes, including unassigned and MISS evidence.

## Comparing target and input ranges

The **Vertical note chart** keeps lanes in their original left-to-right order and places time from top to bottom. Video and chart appear side by side on wide screens, with video on the left and the chart on the right. The video stays visible while scrolling. Narrow screens stack them vertically. **Note size** selects a square size from 8 to 64 CSS pixels (default 24), independent of spacing. If that size would hide lanes or their timing markers, the chart reduces it to fit the available width and labels the effective size. Resizing the window recalculates that fit. **Spacing** changes only pixels per second (0.05× through 16×, in 0.01 steps); use the adjacent numeric field for precise values. The view stays 680 pixels tall. All lanes fit the chart width without horizontal scrolling; compact time labels and panel padding leave more room for the notes. Notes are filled squares in their blue, red or yellow lane color, with a white arrow or center symbol. Square width and height use the effective note size, independently of time spacing. Lime capped lines and midpoint dots to the left mark fitted chart target ranges; magenta capped lines and midpoint dots to the right show input ranges. Separate sides preserve both intervals when their times overlap. Long bodies in lane colors and adjacent magenta recognition spans represent holds separately. Dots are interval midpoints for orientation, never measured subframe switch times. An input above its target is earlier and one below is later. Unconfirmed candidates are hidden in the chart by default. Show candidate connections enables dashed links from individual notes to their eligible input ranges; no generic candidate window is drawn. Conditional neighboring-row bounds remain table/detail evidence and never become chart connections. Chords line up across lanes, and the selected target has a white outline. Gray crosses enabled by the exclusion control show only track endpoints.

The green horizontal line follows video playback, pause and seeking. The chart uses presented video-frame media timestamps when supported, with an animation-frame fallback reading the video clock. Playback speed changes do not create an independent chart clock. All charts and the calibration overlay use SVG, without canvas. The vertical chart retains nearby note, hold and range elements in bounded time pages. Browser-driven CSS transform animations move the retained SVG group; the video-frame callback updates the clock, corrects animation drift and changes pages when necessary. Ordinary frames do not recreate note elements, timing ranges, tables or the overview. Pause, buffering and seeking freeze or reposition the group; playback-rate changes re-anchor its animation. Browsers without the Web Animations API update only the retained group transform. See the [Element.animate API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Element/animate). Use the recording-position slider to scrub and **Previous note / Next note** to inspect supported heads including unmatched ones. Click a target or input outline to select its note and seek the recording. The initial view opens at the first matched target (or first supported target when none match). A five-lane chart and a ten-lane chart use the same left-to-right layout without combining players.

Select a note in the **Note timing comparison** table or lane chart to see its two ranges on the same axis. The target range is the fitted receptor crossing plus/minus its saved prediction uncertainty. The input estimate is the matched combo/judgment observation bracket with the selected display-delay correction subtracted. The table uses absolute recording positions; the selected-note plot uses milliseconds relative to the fitted crossing. Changing the correction updates input ranges, difference intervals and plots together, without moving the target or creating new associations. Tap connections are clipped again to the hard early/late limits at the chosen correction.

The difference includes uncertainty on both sides: for target `[T_low, T_high]` and corrected input `[I_low, I_high]`, it is `[I_low − T_high, I_high − T_low]`. A wholly negative interval establishes an earlier estimate, a wholly positive interval a later estimate, and overlap with zero leaves that distinction unresolved. These are measurement uncertainty ranges, not game judgment windows. This also applies to the overview's offset bars.

Every supported target appears even without a uniquely matched refresh. A separate review pass uses positive combo and non-MISS judgment refreshes within hard limits of 250 ms early and 500 ms late around the fitted target center, after the configured display-delay correction. Prediction uncertainty and contextual ranking offsets cannot widen these limits. Each connected range is the intersection of its original observation bracket and this window; a bracket wholly outside the window cannot connect. This also limits saved tap matches, conditional neighbor bounds and the latency-slider view. Long-note sustained states keep their own duration rather than being treated as repeated tap connections. Original observation brackets and source PTS remain unchanged in evidence; an intersection is a conditional candidate range, not a new measured frame timestamp. The wider search includes processing already visible in neighboring frames. Candidates must respect already-owned refreshes and earlier/later processed rows. Counter, word and raw-reader observations of the same refresh share one row decision. Reliable lane receptor observations reject candidates when only another lane lights up; hold ticks require a separate receptor onset for a tap. Candidate rows are ranked using nearby confirmed display offsets, retaining only near-ties within frame uncertainty. This contextual ranking is not hardware latency calibration. Accepted alternatives retain the supporting lane runs and original source PTS; rejected alternatives retain explicit reason codes. Two neighboring rows can still be alternatives when one frame bracket cannot distinguish them, but they are never counted as two presses. Native associations remain preserved for audit; hold-contaminated matches are demoted in the presented tap view. Distinct ranges remain separate alternatives (shown as a union in the table), with event IDs and exclusion reasons saved in `timing-ranges.json`. For otherwise unresolved notes, saved native counter frames also contribute positive changes of at most ten counts between readable frames at most one second apart, requiring reader confidence of at least 0.8. These retain their original source PTS and remain unconfirmed alternatives; single-frame reads never become confirmed matches. Neighboring confirmed processed rows can also give conditional bounds across gaps of up to three seconds: these assume chart-row input order and do not establish that the intervening note was pressed. The table labels them explicitly. Conditional bounds appear only in the table and selected-note detail, never as magenta input markers in the scrolling chart. A hard ownership, lane or order rejection is not replaced by a conditional guess. If neither evidence exists, the input remains **Unconfirmed**, with the reason retained per note. A persistent judgment word alone does not establish a new input. Possible MISS candidates never receive press times. Shared chord and aggregate row brackets retain their original evidence without inventing individual switch times. Excluded trajectories remain available through the chart's exclusion control and evidence JSON, without fabricated target ranges. No tracked chart catalog is required: original chart-file timestamps are still unavailable, and input estimates remain display-based rather than measured physical switch times.

The raw offset contains input/render/display latency. It is not a measured physical switch time, hardware latency or game judgment-window score. A positive externally calibrated delay is subtracted with:

```sh
just play VIDEO --segment 1 --display-delay-ms 30
```

The HTML slider changes the displayed offsets only; changing associations requires rerunning the command. Median centering is relative alignment, not hardware calibration.

## Variable speed and uncertain motion

The tracker does not assume one scroll speed for the song. It excludes bounding-box centers clipped near the receptor, then fits at most the last twelve unclipped observations of each head. It compares earlier/later slopes with pixel-rounding and measured slope-error tolerance, checks residuals and limits extrapolation to 250 ms and 2.5 lane spacings. Repeated rendered game frames are retained with their original native timestamps; stepwise motion contributes to uncertainty rather than automatically being called acceleration. Predictions bridge gaps up to 120 ms, or 350 ms for established tracks entering the judgment/combo overlay. If the whole fit fails, a sufficiently long linear span after that occlusion may establish a crossing. Same-lane crossing estimates within 35 ms are treated as duplicate colored head fragments; excluded duplicates remain in the evidence. Nonlinear, stopped/reverse, distant and insufficient tracks are excluded. A sufficiently linear portion after a speed change may still be usable. There is no attempt to reconstruct arbitrary gimmicks, disappearing notes or unsupported skins.

## Geometry and limited windows

The current visual detector supports the usual blue diagonal, red diagonal and yellow center note colors, with white outlines and five or ten ordered receptor columns. Whole-head white contours locate centers and color verifies the lane, avoiding fragmented internal colored rings. Auto-calibration compares receptor outlines across source frames. A previously validated layout from the same source can be reused only when at least four current outlines span half its lane grid and match its positions. Complementary partial outlines can establish a grid only when every receiver is directly observed at distinct source timestamps and the complete geometry is unambiguous. If sparse samples repeatedly hit beat flashes, a bounded 800 ms native-frame window supplies differently phased observations before the temporal-median fallback. No missing lane is filled in from presumed spacing. Remaining flashes can be removed using a temporal median of calibration frames. Unknown chart labels do not force five lanes. A different skin, unusual composition or simultaneous players needs a reviewed layout.

For a smaller review window, `--start` is an absolute local playback time and `--duration` limits the analysis window inside that gameplay interval:

```sh
just play VIDEO --segment 1 --start 48 --duration 12
just play VIDEO --segment 1 --layout layout.json --player p2
```

A layout uses game-relative fractions:

```json
{
  "receptor_y": 0.142,
  "note_y_max": 0.95,
  "combo_roi": [0.43, 0.52, 0.14, 0.075],
  "lanes": [
    {"x": 0.38, "color": "blue", "name": "DL"},
    {"x": 0.44, "color": "red", "name": "UL"},
    {"x": 0.50, "color": "yellow", "name": "C"},
    {"x": 0.56, "color": "red", "name": "UR"},
    {"x": 0.62, "color": "blue", "name": "DR"}
  ]
}
```

Do not use the example as calibration for an arbitrary recording. Inspect the overlay and the digit-template source frames. Simultaneous players must be analyzed separately with an explicit player and a verified layout; no values are combined across sides.

## Cost, timestamps and evidence

At most 32 small counter crops receive English OCR to learn digit templates. Subsequent plays can reuse a saved font bank from unchanged source media and inspect up to eight samples, running OCR only on samples the bank cannot read. A complete font from another local recording can also be imported after at least six current crops covering six digit classes match at confidence 0.9 or higher and improve on the local reader by at least three crops. Only glyph images and training provenance transfer; counter values and native timestamps always come from the current recording. Contradictory neural-OCR labels cannot overwrite strongly recognized glyphs. Legacy poorly trained readers can be revalidated against saved bootstrap crops without OCR; only their native counter strips are reread, with original note observations and the original counter baseline preserved. The imported bank is preserved independently; neither counter values nor timing events are transferred between plays. Confirmed counter values can supply additional native-resolution font examples. A recent stable value can restrict visually supported candidates, but blank/unsegmented crops remain unreadable. Temporal-template reads are marked in the frame trace. A bounded recovery pass decodes only the small counter region, trying brighter white thresholds where the original read failed. A recovered value must lie between the nearest original counter readings, within a nondecreasing gap of at most 250 ms and ten increments. Existing readable values, note detections and source PTS are preserved. `frames.base.jsonl` retains the observations before recovery; accepted additions have `combo_recovered: true`. A digit needs examples from distinct source timestamps; repeated glyphs or resized variants from one source do not count as independent confirmation. Native gameplay frames then use color components, visual tracking and digit-template comparisons, without neural OCR on each frame. Unknown glyphs remain unreadable.

FFmpeg streams frames without an FPS filter. Original integer PTS and filter time base determine source time, including nonzero container origins. Resizing is only for analysis; it does not alter media time. `--width` controls analysis resolution (default 650 pixels). Note tracking has no dependency on metadata OCR sampling rate.

Outputs stay under the ignored `.local/` directory:

- `analysis.json`: native tracks, crossing estimates, combo brackets, matches, exclusions and the separate `hold_analysis` reconstruction;
- `frames.jsonl`: native source PTS, playback time, note detections and counter observations per frame;
- `layout.json`, `preview.png`: geometry and its original source image;
- `calibration/`: additional source frames and a temporal median when needed;
- `evidence/`: digit-template training images, font-bank archives, OCR observations and exact source timestamps;
- `report.html`: local interactive presentation;
- `holds.json`, `hold-features.json`, `hold-frames.jsonl`: long-note reconstruction and reusable native pixel observations;
- `timing-ranges.json`: disposable per-note target ranges, matched brackets, alternative refreshes, conditional bounds and unresolved reasons. It is derived from saved analysis without OCR or frame decoding.

Changing source metadata, play bounds, geometry, resolution, player or song/chart invalidates the observation cache. Compatible saved native observations can be refitted after a tracking/association change or latency-correction change without decoding or OCR. A counter-reader upgrade may decode only the small counter region and must match every saved source PTS; force a complete rebuild with `--force`. This feature is experimental: verify representative frames and report coverage before drawing conclusions about a player.

## Cache compatibility

Cache reuse also checks saved counter bootstrap crops against newly available local font banks. An incomplete reader can be invalidated by a newly available font only after the same broad pixel-validation requirements pass. The check does not mutate the cache or decode media. A successful replacement rereads only the native counter strip; note observations and source timestamps stay unchanged. A complete, locally validated reader does not depend on unrelated new recordings.

Sparse bootstrap crops can reuse a font already broadly pixel-validated in another play of the exact same media identity, game ROI and analysis width. This requires two newly matching crops covering five digit classes at confidence 0.9 or higher and at least two more strong reads than the weak local reader. Changed recordings or geometry still require broad validation. A sparse within-recording import cannot qualify as the broad donor for another sparse import.

Lane onset support retains an explicit bracket from the last observed unlit receptor to the first sustained lane-local light. A common screen flash may obscure the intermediate samples for at most 150 ms, provided adjacent native observations remain within 75 ms. The bracket retains both original source PTS. Unknown samples remain unknown; persistent light, an unknown initial state, unrelated unreadable samples and gaps in decoded observations cannot establish a new onset. This support can corroborate an independently observed display refresh; it does not measure a physical switch or turn a hold tick into a press.

One lane transition cannot independently confirm multiple repeated taps. If several matched notes on that lane claim the same onset bracket, their hold-time associations remain ambiguous unless each has a separate unclaimed transition. Neighboring display refreshes remain available as review candidates.

Strict counter-to-row alignments that overlap a possible hold/dense interval are retained as deferred matches. A separate lane-local onset can restore a tap association after hold geometry is known. Recovery requires a single counter increment, a visible tap rather than a hold component, an unshared onset and no conflicting matched row at the same refresh. The original counter bracket is preserved. Aggregate jumps and sustained receptor light remain insufficient to recover a press.

Cache compatibility is checked by artifact format and dependencies, not by a list of analyzer releases. Pixel features record their encoding and source/ROI/time/width identity in `evidence/judgments/features.json`. Font files declare their binary-mask format. Reader output records the feature identity, font-content fingerprint and reader revision; associations additionally depend on notes, counter evidence, unsupported intervals, display correction and association revision. Changing association logic reuses saved word observations. Changing the reader reuses compatible pixel features and fonts. Source or feature-format changes reacquire pixels.

Untagged legacy feature/font files are validated and normalized at the load boundary. Unknown formats are rejected. Native note traces are validated for normalized coordinates and original timestamps. Song spelling and level corrections update report metadata without reacquiring the same gameplay frames; a changed single/double layout requires compatible lane geometry. If metadata inserts a play and renumbers later reports, immutable retained snapshots are matched by original media, playback interval, ROI, width, player and layout. Copied evidence paths are relocated into the new report directory.
